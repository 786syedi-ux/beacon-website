What PE-backed HR teams get wrong about scale

August 2026·Beacon

Since 2018, the share of PE-owned portfolio companies with a Chief Human Resources Officer — or an equivalent formal HR partner — has risen from near zero to over 50%, according to Wellington Management's research on human capital management in private companies. Sponsors have clearly concluded that HR belongs in the value creation conversation. What's less settled is what that HR function should actually be doing in year one.

Most 100-day plans we see cover the predictable ground: org chart, headcount plan, compensation benchmarking against the new investment thesis. What they consistently miss is leadership bench depth and manager capability — which is exactly where AlixPartners' research points: 75% of portfolio company leaders name talent retention as their single biggest challenge, and 67% of PE investors point to talent recruitment as their primary hurdle. Both are downstream of the same root cause — not enough ready leaders for the roles growth is about to create.

This is where scaling companies get caught out. The org chart tells you what roles will exist in eighteen months. It says nothing about whether the person who was a great individual contributor at 40 employees is ready to manage a team of twelve at 150 — and by the time that gap becomes visible, it's usually already cost the company a quarter of missed execution.

What a genuine 100-day people diagnostic should cover, beyond the org chart: an honest leadership readiness assessment against where the company will be in 18 months, not where it is today; a compensation and role architecture review that will still make sense at 3x current headcount; and a short list of the two or three manager-development gaps most likely to slow execution first.

The org chart is necessary. It was never sufficient.
