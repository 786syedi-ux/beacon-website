What the sharpest healthcare cost rise in 15 years means for your 2026 renewal

August 2026·Beacon

Average health benefit cost per employee is projected to rise 6.7% in 2026, according to Mercer's latest National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans — the sharpest increase in 15 years. It follows a 6% rise in 2025, when average family premiums reached $26,993, according to KFF's Employer Health Benefits Survey. Two consecutive years of unusually steep increases is no longer a blip; it's a trend line.

A meaningful share of the increase is coming from specialty and prescription drug spend — GLP-1 medications in particular — layered on top of rising chronic disease rates. Neither of those drivers is going to reverse on its own timeline, which means employers who treat cost review as a once-a-year renewal conversation are consistently a step behind it.

The organisations managing this well aren't necessarily paying less — they're deciding earlier. That means modelling next year's cost trend 12–18 months out, not 8 weeks before renewal; separating the decision into three distinct levers (plan design, provider network strategy, and member-level prevention) rather than defaulting to the blunt instrument of raising deductibles; and being explicit with employees about what's changing and why, since unexplained cost-shifting is what actually damages trust, not the cost itself.

A practical starting point: before your next renewal cycle opens, benchmark your current plan design and cost trend against comparable organisations, and model at least two alternative plan structures — not just the renewal your broker sends you by default.
